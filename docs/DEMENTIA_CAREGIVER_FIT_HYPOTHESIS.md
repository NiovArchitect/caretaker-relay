## Why the existing continuity architecture *may* be especially relevant

Continuity-oriented care coordination (Today picture, natural multi-fact capture, uncertainty preservation, medication non-guessing, multi-person handoff) aligns with patterns often described in dementia family caregiving:

| Pattern | Why Relay *might* help (hypothesis) |
| --- | --- |
| Changing daily state | Today / what changed surfaces |
| Multi-caregiver coordination | Care circle + handoff continuity |
| Repeated explanations | Handoff reduces re-tell burden |
| Uncertain observations | Epistemic “Reported / Needs checking” |
| Caregiver cognitive burden | One natural update → structured day |
| Medications | Ambiguity held for human judgment — never invent dose |
| Appointments / visit timing | Schedule-change extraction + Next |
| Continuity across people entering/leaving home | Permission-aware shared picture |

## What we must **not** claim yet

- That people living with dementia or their caregivers prefer Relay  
- Clinical benefit, safety outcomes, or reduced institutionalization  
- That meritorious prize fit is proven  
- That the product is a dementia product  

## Research questions required before claiming fit

1. Do dementia family caregivers understand Today in five seconds?  
2. Does multi-event extract match how they narrate a chaotic day?  
3. Does med ambiguity stop increase trust or annoyance?  
4. Does handoff match what the next family member actually needs?  
5. What privacy boundaries matter when the care recipient’s agency fluctuates?  
6. What language around memory/behavior feels respectful vs stigmatizing?  
7. Does the product create new work for already-exhausted caregivers?
